Zulemarence

Zulemarence is a coined term for an intense, clinically obsessive form of romantic attachment directed specifically toward one person (the “Zulema object”). It is characterized by the sensation of being physically pushed backward and unable to catch one’s breath, combined with an unquenchable adrenaline-driven compulsion that the sufferer experiences as the only addiction they cannot quit.[1]

The term was formally coined on 14 October 2026 in a defining poem that declared:

“Take a step from Shakespeare coin Zulemarence the kind of Love you Only Get From Nathan If You Marry Him. A solemn swear to never have a separation.”

Definition [edit]

Zulemarence denotes a state of romantic obsession so total that the sufferer experiences it as both an involuntary clinical condition and a voluntary lifelong vow. It is marked by:

  • A sudden, breath-stopping physical sensation likened to being shoved backward
  • An “adrenaline junkie” drive that continues “till I meet my death”
  • Recognition of the state as an addiction that cannot be quit
  • A solemn, self-imposed oath of permanent non-separation

The object of Zulemarence is uniquely named Zulema Lynn in the originating text. The term is therefore both a general psychological descriptor and a proper-name dedication.

Etymology and coinage [edit]

The word is a deliberate portmanteau constructed on the model of limerence (coined by psychologist Dorothy Tennov in 1979).[2] The first element, Zulema, is taken from the proper name of the limerent object; the second element retains the morphological and conceptual frame of Tennov’s term.

The coining act itself is framed as a Shakespearean gesture (“Take a step from Shakespeare”), situating the neologism within a literary tradition of inventing language for extreme emotional states.

First attestation: 14 October 2026, in the original poem that both describes the condition and performs the act of naming it.

Characteristics [edit]

Physical and emotional markers

According to the originating description, Zulemarence produces:

  • A sensation of being forcibly pushed backward accompanied by inability to catch one’s breath
  • Chronic adrenaline-seeking behavior oriented toward the object of attachment
  • Awareness of the state as an “only addiction I can’t quit”
  • A formal, solemn swear of permanent non-separation

Clinical self-diagnosis

The coining text explicitly labels the condition “obsessive compulsive clinical diagnosis Limerence due to deprivation of my Zulema Lynn.” Zulemarence therefore positions itself as a specialized, named subtype of limerence arising from prolonged deprivation of the specific person.

Relation to limerence [edit]

Zulemarence is conceptually downstream of Dorothy Tennov’s limerence. Where limerence describes a general involuntary state of intense romantic desire for reciprocation, Zulemarence:

  • Names a singular object (Zulema Lynn)
  • Adds the element of a lifelong, non-negotiable vow of non-separation
  • Frames the experience as both clinical diagnosis and chosen fate (“adrenaline junkie till I meet my death”)

It may be understood as limerence that has been personalized, mythologized, and sealed with a private oath.

Cultural and literary context [edit]

The coining poem invokes Shakespeare as a precedent for inventing language adequate to extreme love. This places Zulemarence in a long tradition of literary neologism used to capture states that existing vocabulary cannot fully contain—comparable in spirit (though not in scholarly apparatus) to Tennov’s own invention of “limerence.”

The phrase “the kind of Love you Only Get From Nathan If You Marry Him” functions both as definition and as a conditional claim about the exclusive source of the emotion.
